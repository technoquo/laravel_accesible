## What it requires

All functionality must be operable through a keyboard, without requiring specific timings between keystrokes. The only exception is functionality that depends on the path of movement, such as freehand drawing.

## Why it matters

Many people do not use a mouse: blind screen reader users, people with tremors or limited mobility, and people using switches or voice control (which emulate the keyboard). If something only works with a mouse, it does not exist for them.

## How to meet it in Laravel

Use native elements: `<button>` for actions and `<a href>` for navigation. They are already focusable and activate with `Enter` (buttons also with `Space`).

```blade
{{-- ❌ Cannot be focused or activated with the keyboard --}}
<div wire:click="delete({{ $post->id }})">Delete</div>

{{-- ✅ --}}
<button type="button" wire:click="delete({{ $post->id }})">Delete</button>
```

The same applies to Alpine: `x-on:click` on a `<div>` does not work with the keyboard; put it on a `<button>`.

If a custom component needs special keys (tabs, menus with arrow keys), implement the keyboard pattern from the ARIA Authoring Practices Guide:

```blade
<div role="tablist" aria-label="Content language"
     x-on:keydown.right.prevent="$focus.wrap().next()"
     x-on:keydown.left.prevent="$focus.wrap().previous()">
    ...
</div>
```

(`$focus` comes from Alpine's Focus plugin.)

Drag-and-drop features, such as `wire:sort`, need an alternative with buttons ("Move up", "Move down").

## Common mistakes

- `wire:click` or `x-on:click` on elements that are neither buttons nor links.
- Menus that only open on mouse hover.
- Scrollable areas that cannot be focused to scroll them with the arrow keys.
- `tabindex="-1"` on controls that should receive focus.

## How to test it

- **Manual:** put the mouse aside and go through the page with `Tab`, `Shift + Tab`, `Enter`, `Space`, the arrow keys and `Escape`. Every action must be completable.
- **Automated:** axe detects some cases (`scrollable-region-focusable`, `frame-focusable-content`), but it cannot tell whether a `<div>` with `wire:click` should be a button.
